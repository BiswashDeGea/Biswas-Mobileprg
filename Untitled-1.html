how it works

A day of your work, for a day of theirs.

Parma is how villages across Nepal have always shared labor — planting, harvesting, roofing — without money changing hands. This app just keeps the tally, so the exchange stays fair even months later.

See how it works Open the ledger

Parma — a Nepali tradition of reciprocal labor exchange, common in farming villages, where neighbors take turns working each other's fields, roofs, or houses instead of paying wages.

How an exchange happens

1

Post what you need

"Planting maize, need 3 people, one day, next Tuesday." Anyone nearby can see it.

2

Neighbors offer their day

People who can help mark themselves in. No payment is discussed — the work itself is the currency.

3

The ledger remembers

Every day given or received is logged, so favors don't get forgotten — and nobody keeps quietly score in their head.

Your balance, at a glance

Every household has a running ledger — days owed, and days still to give back. No numbers, no money. Just work, honestly tracked.

Entries are added by whoever showed up, and confirmed by the household that was helped.
